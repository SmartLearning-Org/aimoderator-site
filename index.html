01 / The approach

Your learning goals.
Your approach to moderation.

Learning through dialogue means explaining, questioning and building on other people’s ideas. Shape the moderator’s role around the kind of academic exchange you want to encourage.

Set the purpose.
Shape the participation.

Shared moderation profiles define the purpose, instructions and timing of support. Teachers select a profile for their activity. The moderator uses that approach to assess the discussion and contribute in the shared conversation. Uncertainty and respectful disagreement remain normal parts of learning.

What the moderator pays attention to

The selected profile guides five specialised assessments: interpersonal conflict or expressed distress, discussion rules, participation, reasoning and timing. Administrators set their instructions and the timing of assessments. With automatic replies enabled, an assessment can lead to a contribution in the shared conversation. A time threshold prompts an assessment, not an automatic reply.

AI Moderator supports live academic discussions. Administrators define how the moderator supports learning across activities.

Start in People, where you create teacher accounts and organise access.

Next, prepare shared moderation profiles. Give each profile a clear purpose, such as comparing evidence or exploring contrasting perspectives.

Set the learning objective, discussion rules and conversation language.

Choose whether automatic moderation publishes contributions, records decisions for observation, or is switched off.

Participation and timing settings shape when support is considered, including planned pauses.

Teachers then select a profile for their dialogue and set up the activity.

Together, administrators and teachers determine how much, how and when the moderator participates. Students develop and discuss their own ideas.

AI Moderator lets you design a live discussion around your learning goals. You shape the activity and choose how AI supports it.

Select a shared moderation profile prepared by your administrator. This sets the moderator’s approach, instructions and timing.

Give the dialogue a topic and a clear task. Here, students compare nuclear power within a low-carbon electricity system.

Set the start and end times. Choose the number of participants and groups.

Share an individual join code with each student. Each code connects them to their group.

During the activity, view its status, groups and dialogue details. You can end the dialogue when needed.

The moderator participates according to the selected profile. Students can also request help in the shared conversation.

A few useful things to know

What kind of discussion is it designed for?+

Both synchronous and asynchronous group discussions around a shared question or task. Students can participate at the same time or contribute at different times, developing their ideas and responding to peers in a shared dialogue. They can also request the moderator’s help.

Students can read, listen and reflect without having to keep typing or posting.

How much control do educators have?+

Administrators maintain shared moderation profiles: learning goals, discussion rules, language, timing and the instructions for the five assessments.

Teachers choose a profile to match their learning activity, set the topic and task, schedule the dialogue and organise groups. They can view the activity’s status and end it when needed.

Participants can request support during the discussion by typing @moderator or selecting Call Moderator. The moderator responds in the shared conversation.

Does the AI replace the educator?+

No. It offers support within the group conversation. Educators remain responsible for the learning design and for reviewing how the activity works.
